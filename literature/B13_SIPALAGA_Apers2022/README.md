# B13: SIPALAGA stations listed by Apers 2022 (59 stations)

**Status:** No public data

**Data source:** [SIPALAGA portal (BRGM)](https://sipalaga.brgm.go.id)

**Paper:** Apers et al. 2022, JAMES 14, https://doi.org/10.1029/2021MS002784 (Open; saved in D9_PEATCLSM_Trop_Apers2022/paper/)

> Data availability: SIPALAGA 'real-time (daily, hourly or sub-hourly) water level data'; sites manually quality-checked and classed natural or drained from Google Earth.

**Paper:** Putra et al. 2025, Indonesian Journal of Environmental Management and Sustainability 9, https://doi.org/10.26554/ijems.2025.9.2.46-55 (Open; downloaded to paper/)

> Data availability: No data availability statement; data 'obtained from 39 monitoring stations operated under SIPALAGA'.

**Paper:** Hein et al. 2022, Regional Environmental Change 22, https://doi.org/10.1007/s10113-022-01979-z (Open; downloaded to paper/)

> Data availability: 'We downloaded from the website of the Peat Restoration Agency ... (https://sipalaga.brg.go.id) the daily water levels of 167 water monitoring stations, over the complete year 2019.'

**How the paper used the data:** Hein 2022 downloaded daily water levels of 167 SIPALAGA stations for 2019 and averaged the 25 oil palm stations in East Sumatra (mean drainage depth 81 +/- 50 cm; 10 of 25 at 60 cm or less) to drive a subsidence and flood-risk model. Apers 2022 used 59 SIPALAGA stations (2019-2020) to evaluate PEATCLSM_Trop (D9). Putra 2025 analysed daily rainfall and peat water level at 39 SIPALAGA stations in Riau's peat hydrological units (Rokan, Siak, Kampar, ...), October 2018 - December 2020; dry-period water tables reached -0.6 to -0.8 m.

- sipalaga.brgm.go.id returns a Cloudflare block page to the cloud; try it from your browser.
- Two South Sumatra stations (Cinta Jaya, IN_BRG_160224_01/_02) match the coordinates of Irfan 2023's OKI-1/OKI-2 (B9).
